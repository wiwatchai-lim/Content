## A. Brief keys in scope (verification)

### MullerBrockmann1981
- **Title:** Grid Systems in Graphic Design / Raster Systeme für die visuelle Gestaltung
- **Author / year / publisher:** Josef Müller-Brockmann, 1981, Verlag Arthur Niggli (Teufen/Sulgen, CH). Bilingual EN/DE. Many later revised printings (4th 1996, 6th 2008, 10th 2016).
- **ID:** ISBN 978-3-7212-0145-1. Publisher page https://niggli.ch/en/grid-systems-in-graphic-design.html. Texas A&M catalogue record lists 1981, Niggli + Hastings House.
- **Evidence type:** Bibliographic. Practitioner (Swiss modernist design manual).
- **Accessed:** search results for the publisher page, library records and bookseller listings (176 pp., 357 ill.). Book text **not read**.
- **Usable findings (from the listings only):** the book is a manual of modular grids for print, exhibitions and three-dimensional design. Use it as the canonical reference for "column + module + gutter + margin" layouts.
- **Limitations:** bibliographic only. Do not attribute specific rules or page numbers to it. Key verified; the brief's year (1981) matches the first edition.

### Bringhurst2004
- **Title:** The Elements of Typographic Style, 3rd ed. (version 3.0)
- **Author / year / publisher:** Robert Bringhurst, 2004, Hartley & Marks (Vancouver). Version 3.1 is dated 2005 with the same ISBN; 4th ed. (v4.0) 2012.
- **ID:** ISBN 0-88179-206-3 (pbk), 0-88179-205-5 (hc). Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Elements_of_Typographic_Style ; webtypography.net §2.1.2 (Rutter's web adaptation of Bringhurst) https://webtypography.net/2.1.2
- **Evidence type:** Bibliographic. Practitioner.
- **Accessed:** catalogue and bookseller records (version 3.0 title page with © 1992, 1996, 2004), plus secondary sources quoting the measure passage. Book text **not read**.
- **Usable findings (secondary):** (1) a comfortable single-column measure is 45–75 characters, with 66 (letters and spaces) the ideal, cited to pp. 25–26. (2) For multi-column work about 40–50 characters is suggested.
- **Limitations:** the 2004/2005 version confusion is real. Measure figures are Latin text conventions: Thai has no word spaces, so a character count means something different there (see Thai rules).

### McCloud1993
- **Title:** Understanding Comics: The Invisible Art
- **Author / year / publisher:** Scott McCloud, 1993 (Tundra / Kitchen Sink Press). 1994 paperback, William Morrow / HarperPerennial (HarperCollins).
- **ID:** ISBN 0-06-097625-X / 978-0060976255. Wikipedia https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Understanding_Comics ; summaries at EBSCO Research Starters and MediaSmarts.
- **Evidence type:** Bibliographic. Practitioner (comics theory written as a comic).
- **Accessed:** library records (Simmons, UNT, Open Library), study-guide summaries. Book text **not read**.
- **Usable findings (secondary):** (1) "closure": the reader fills the gap between panels to infer what happened. (2) Six panel-to-panel transitions: moment-to-moment, action-to-action, subject-to-subject, scene-to-scene, aspect-to-aspect, non sequitur. (3) McCloud reports Japanese comics using subject-to-subject and aspect-to-aspect transitions much more than US comics. Aspect-to-aspect gives a slow, mood-building rhythm.
- **Limitations:** the taxonomy is contested. Academic work (e.g. Cohn, Tilburg) argues that linear panel-to-panel analysis is not enough for many sequences. Treat it as a planning vocabulary, not a law.

### Block2008
- **Title:** The Visual Story: Creating the Visual Structure of Film, TV and Digital Media, 2nd ed.
- **Author / year / publisher:** Bruce Block, 2008 (© 2008, retail release Oct 2007), Focal Press / Elsevier. 3rd ed. Routledge 2020.
- **ID:** ISBN 978-0-240-80779-9. O'Reilly chapter "Contrast and Affinity" https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-visual-story/9780240807799/009_9780080551692_chapter2.html (returned 403 to fetch).
- **Evidence type:** Bibliographic. Practitioner.
- **Accessed:** search snippets of the O'Reilly preview and the publisher listing. Book text **not read**.
- **Usable findings (snippets):** (1) the "principle of contrast and affinity" applies to all of the book's basic visual components (space, line, shape, tone, colour, movement, rhythm). Contrast (difference) raises visual intensity; affinity (similarity) lowers it. (2) Tone is the worked example: maximum tonal contrast means extreme brightness differences on a grey scale.
- **Limitations:** the component list and the intensity framing come from the snippets and the well-known structure of the book, not from a reading of it. Verify before quoting.

## B. Graphic design, layout, typography, hierarchy, brand, platform specs

### Lupton2024
- **Title:** Thinking with Type: A Critical Guide for Designers, Writers, Editors, and Students, 3rd ed. revised and expanded
- **Author / year / publisher:** Ellen Lupton, 2024 (12 Mar), Princeton Architectural Press. 2nd ed. 2010.
- **ID:** ISBN 978-1-7972-2682-8. Publisher page https://papress.com/products/thinking-with-type-3-edition
- **Evidence type:** Bibliographic. Practitioner.
- **Accessed:** publisher and library listings (256 pp., 32 new pages). thinkingwithtype.com failed to load. Book text **not read**.
- **Usable findings (standard definitions, shown in secondary typography sources):** leading = baseline-to-baseline line distance. Tracking = uniform spacing across a range of letters. Kerning = spacing adjusted between a specific pair. Values are usually in thousandths of an em.
- **Limitations:** the definitions above are industry-standard. They are not quoted from Lupton.

### Tufte1983
- **Title:** The Visual Display of Quantitative Information (2nd ed. 2001)
- **Author / year / publisher:** Edward R. Tufte, 1983, Graphics Press (Cheshire, CT).
- **ID:** Europa data-viz guide on chartjunk/data-ink origins https://data.europa.eu/apps/data-visualisation-guide/chart-junk-and-data-ink-origins ; practitioner perspectives on chartjunk https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.02634
- **Evidence type:** Bibliographic. Practitioner. The critique paper is Empirical/qualitative.
- **Accessed:** secondary summaries and the arXiv critique abstract. Book **not read**.
- **Usable findings:** (1) "data-ink" is the non-erasable core of a graphic. Tufte's data-ink ratio is data-ink divided by total ink, and his advice is to maximize it. (2) "Chartjunk" is decoration that isn't needed to understand the data. (3) Later practitioners and researchers (Few, Holmes; arXiv 2009.02634) dispute strict minimalism, since embellishment can help memorability.
- **Limitations:** applies to info-graphics and carousels with numbers, not to mood images.

### NNG-Pernice2017
- **Title:** F-Shaped Pattern of Reading on the Web: Misunderstood, But Still Relevant (Even on Mobile)
- **Author / year / venue:** Kara Pernice, 12 Nov 2017 (reviewed 2026), Nielsen Norman Group.
- **URL:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/
- **Evidence type:** Empirical (eyetracking heatmaps of about 45–47 participants per page, plus mobile gaze replays). Practitioner write-up.
- **Accessed:** full article.
- **Usable findings:** (1) The F-pattern appears when a page is a wall of text, the user wants to be efficient, and the user is not committed. It is "the default pattern when there are no strong cues." (2) It mirrors in right-to-left languages. (3) The fix is strong cues: informative first words, clear headings, bold key terms and bullets. Other patterns are layer-cake, spotted, marking (common on mobile), bypassing and commitment.
- **Limitations:** this is text-heavy web content, not single social images. Thai is left-to-right, so the F-orientation applies, but the study did not test Thai.

### NNG-Pernice2019
- **Title:** The Layer-Cake Pattern of Scanning Content on the Web
- **Author / year / venue:** Kara Pernice, 4 Aug 2019, Nielsen Norman Group.
- **URL:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/ (overview of all four patterns at https://www.nngroup.com/articles/text-scanning-patterns-eyetracking/)
- **Evidence type:** Empirical (eyetracking). Practitioner write-up.
- **Accessed:** full article (the overview article only via search snippet).
- **Usable findings:** (1) With visually distinct, descriptive headings, users fixate mostly on headings. This is the most effective scanning method short of reading every word. (2) Make subheads stand out consistently, but not so much that they look like ads. (3) Use proximity and spacing so each label clearly belongs to its content.
- **Limitations:** the article does not discuss the Z-pattern (see Unverified).

## C. Thai script, Thai typography, Thai fonts

### Unicode18-Ch16
- **Title:** The Unicode Standard, Version 18.0 — Core Specification, Chapter 16 (South and Southeast Asia), §16.1 Thai
- **Org / year:** Unicode Consortium, v18.0.
- **URL:** https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode18.0.0/core-spec/chapter-16/
- **Evidence type:** Standard/spec.
- **Accessed:** Thai section (via fetch summary).
- **Usable findings:** (1) The Thai block is U+0E00–U+0E7F. Left-side vowels (เ แ โ ใ ไ) are stored **before** the consonant (visual order), unlike most Indic scripts. (2) Above marks are displayed outward from the base in text order. Tone marks (U+0E48–U+0E4B) render above other above-marks, and SARA AM's nikhahit goes below a tone mark. (3) Thai words are not separated by spaces; spaces mark phrase or sentence boundaries. U+200B ZERO WIDTH SPACE marks an explicit break opportunity and is ignored in justification.
- **Limitations:** encoding and rendering level. No guidance on aesthetics or line height.

### W3C-ThaiLreq2026
- **Title:** Thai Script Resources (formerly "Thai Layout Requirements"), W3C Group Note Draft
- **Editor / year:** Richard Ishida (W3C Internationalization WG), 20 Mar 2026. Contributors include Ben Mitchell, Martin Hosken.
- **URL:** https://www.w3.org/TR/thai-lreq/ (companion Thai Gap Analysis https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/DNOTE-thai-gap-20240710/)
- **Evidence type:** Standard/spec (draft note, not a W3C Recommendation).
- **Accessed:** full page.
- **Usable findings:** (1) Composite vowels may use up to four glyphs plus a tone mark, around the base on up to three sides. (2) "Spaces separate phrases, rather than words." (3) Line height, justification and punctuation sections are still mostly open questions ("tbc") that link out.
- **Limitations:** draft and incomplete. It gives no numeric line-height rule. Use it as an index of issues.

### Ishida-ThaiOrthography
- **Title:** Thai Orthography Notes
- **Author / year:** Richard Ishida, updated 16 May 2026, r12a.github.io.
- **URL:** https://r12a.github.io/scripts/thai/th.html
- **Evidence type:** Practitioner/reference (W3C i18n lead's script notes).
- **Accessed:** full page (fetch summary).
- **Usable findings:** (1) There are 41 basic consonants, 5 pre-base vowels, 8 above/below combining vowel marks and 4 tone marks (่ ้ ๊ ๋) for 5 tones. Vowel and tone marks can stack above one base, and typing them in the wrong order gives a visibly misplaced mark. (2) Line breaks must fall at word boundaries, found by dictionary or heuristics; browsers vary. Authors can force breaks with U+200B and prevent them with U+2060 WORD JOINER. (3) ๆ (mai yamok) repeats the preceding word, is normally surrounded by spaces and must not wrap alone onto a new line. ฯ marks abbreviation (กรุงเทพฯ) and is followed by a space. ์ (thanthakhat/karan) silences a consonant (คอมพิวเตอร์).
- **Limitations:** a reference note, not a typesetting standard.

### MS-ThaiOpenType
- **Title:** Developing OpenType Fonts for Thai Script
- **Org / year:** Microsoft Typography (Learn), 2018 (updated 2022).
- **URL:** https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/typography/script-development/thai
- **Evidence type:** Standard/spec (shaping-engine documentation).
- **Accessed:** full page.
- **Usable findings:** (1) There are up to **four above-levels**: ABOVE1 (ั ิ ี ึ ื), ABOVE2 (็ ํ), ABOVE3 (tone marks ่ ้ ๊ ๋), ABOVE4 (์ ๎). There are two below-levels: BELOW1 (ุ ู), BELOW2 (ฺ). The vertical budget above the x-height is therefore large. (2) Invalid mark combinations render on a dotted circle ◌. If a ◌ appears in a render, the text has a typo or the font is wrong. (3) Fonts need 'mark'/'mkmk' positioning and 'ccmp' (e.g. decompose SARA AM, remove ญ's tail before a below-vowel). A font without these features produces colliding or floating marks.
- **Limitations:** for font developers. Uniscribe-specific details may differ from HarfBuzz (Chrome).

### ICU-BoundaryAnalysis
- **Title:** ICU User Guide — Boundary Analysis
- **Org:** Unicode ICU project.
- **URL:** https://unicode-org.github.io/icu/userguide/boundaryanalysis/
- **Evidence type:** Standard/spec (library documentation).
- **Accessed:** full page.
- **Usable findings:** (1) "ICU provides dictionary support for word boundaries in Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese." (2) Dictionary use is automatic and needs no extra API. Browsers built on ICU (Chrome) wrap Thai at dictionary word boundaries, which can still be wrong for names, brand words and loanwords.
- **Limitations:** dictionary quality decides break quality. Novel words may be split badly, so check every render.

### GoogleFonts-Metrics
- **Title:** Google Fonts Guide — Vertical Metrics
- **Org:** Google Fonts (gf-guide).
- **URL:** https://googlefonts.github.io/gf-guide/metrics.html
- **Evidence type:** Standard/spec (foundry onboarding spec).
- **Accessed:** full page.
- **Usable findings:** (1) Total vertical metrics should be about 130% of UPM; "tall scripts" may need more. (2) WinAscent/WinDescent must cover the tallest and deepest glyphs, or glyphs are clipped. USE_TYPO_METRICS lets the Typo values set line spacing. (3) The guide warns that its Latin-focused rules "probably will not fully work for other complex scripts".
- **Limitations:** Thai is not mentioned explicitly. The link to stacked Thai marks is inference, backed by MS-ThaiOpenType's four above-levels.

### MangasAfonso2025
- **Title:** The Contemporary Effects of Thai Loops: A Perceptual Study
- **Authors / year / venue:** Héctor Mangas Afonso & Mint Tantisuwanna. ATypI Copenhagen 2025 (talk, 24 Apr 2025). Study run in Bangkok, Feb 2024 (Chulalongkorn), commissioned around Typotheque's Thai fonts.
- **URL:** https://atypi.org/?p=34632
- **Evidence type:** Empirical (conference abstract; three experiments: reading acuity, reading speed, perception).
- **Accessed:** abstract page only.
- **Usable findings:** (1) In the abstract's summary, loops did not change reading performance. (2) All age groups **rated looped fonts as more legible**, especially for information-dense media (books, websites). (3) Age significantly affected performance, and **younger readers preferred loopless at small sizes**.
- **Limitations:** a conference abstract, not peer-reviewed. Sample size is not on the page. The companion Typotheque article below reports a "significant" paragraph-speed advantage for looped type, which conflicts with "did not change reading performance". Treat the effect on speed as small or uncertain.

### Typotheque2025
- **Title:** Bridging Cultures Through Letters: Typotheque's Typographic Journey in Thailand
- **Author / year / venue:** content supplied by Typotheque (promotional), GRANSHAN, 14 Aug 2025.
- **URL:** https://granshan.com/insights/bridging-cultures-through-letters-typotheques-typographic-journey-in-thailand

### Punsongserm2017
- **Title:** Thai typefaces (Part 2): Criticism based on legibility test of some isolated characters
- **Authors / year / venue:** Rachapoom Punsongserm, Shoji Sunaga, Hisayasu Ihara. *Archives of Design Research* 30(2), 23–44, 2017.
- **DOI:** 10.15187/adr.2017.05.30.2.23
- **Evidence type:** Empirical (pilot legibility test).
- **Accessed:** abstract (Kyushu University repository page).
- **Usable findings:** (1) 50 conventional Thai text fonts were tested at equal character heights under Gaussian blur that simulates low vision. (2) Five design factors for a Thai universal-design typeface: type anatomy, character width, character shape, stroke shapes and terminals. Wider, open shapes help under blur.
- **Limitations:** isolated characters, not words. Blur is a proxy for low vision and distance.

### Punsongserm2025
- **Title:** Comparing legibility of Thai Universal Design typeface and Roman-like Thai typefaces using multi-viewing-distance threshold
- **Author / year / venue:** Rachapoom Punsongserm. *Grafica* (UAB) v14 n27, 2025. Presented at ATypI Brisbane 2024.
- **URL:** https://revistes.uab.cat/grafica/article/view/v14-n27-punsongserm ; DOAJ https://doaj.org/article/1b53ef276fcd44adba5c15bcff59a8dc
- **Evidence type:** Empirical.
- **Accessed:** abstract via search and DOAJ/ATypI snippets (not the full paper).
- **Usable findings:** (1) 31 Thai participants of varied ages and acuity identified 36 consonants at 15 viewing distances. ### Liu2023
- **Title:** Character-Aware Models Improve Visual Text Rendering
- **Authors / year / venue:** Rosanne Liu, Dan Garrette, Chitwan Saharia, William Chan, Adam Roberts, Sharan Narang, Irina Blok, RJ Mical, Mohammad Norouzi, Noah Constant. ACL 2023 (Long), 16270–16297.
- **DOI / URL:** 10.18653/v1/2023.acl-long.900 ; arXiv:2212.10562
- **Evidence type:** Empirical (ML).
- **Accessed:** abstract (ACL Anthology / Google Research listing).
- **Usable findings:** (1) Popular text-to-image models use character-blind (subword) text encoders, so they struggle to spell words as glyph sequences. (2) Character-aware encoders improve visual spelling a lot (30+ points on rare words). This is the mechanism behind AI images misspelling text. It is worse for scripts that are rare in training data, and for Thai with its stacked marks: always set Thai in the HTML layer.
- **Limitations:** English-centred benchmarks. No Thai-specific measurement. The Thai claim is an inference.

## E. Character design, expression, cuteness, uncanny valley

### EkmanFriesen1978
- **Title:** Facial Action Coding System (FACS) manual
- **Authors / year / publisher:** Paul Ekman & Wallace V. Friesen, 1978 (Consulting Psychologists Press). Revised 2002 with Joseph C. Hager.
- **URL:** https://www.paulekman.com/facial-action-coding-system/
- **Evidence type:** Standard (scientific coding system). Publisher page.
- **Accessed:** Paul Ekman Group page (full).
- **Usable findings:** (1) FACS is "a comprehensive, anatomically based system for describing all visually discernible facial movement". Expressions decompose into Action Units (single muscle movements). (2) The 2002 revision is the one to use. Certification takes about 50–100 hours of self-study. For art direction, describe expressions as muscle actions (brow inner raise, lip-corner pull, lid tighten) rather than adjectives alone.
- **Limitations:** the CPP publisher detail was not confirmed on that page. FACS describes movement and does not by itself say which emotion is felt.

### EkmanFriesen1971
- **Title:** Constants across cultures in the face and emotion
- **Authors / year / venue:** Paul Ekman & Wallace V. Friesen, *J. Personality and Social Psychology* 17(2), 124–129, 1971.
- **DOI:** 10.1037/h0030377 ; PMID 5542557 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5542557/
- **Evidence type:** Empirical.
- **Accessed:** PubMed record (no abstract) and secondary course summaries.
- **Usable findings:** (1) Preliterate Fore participants (Papua New Guinea) matched posed expressions to emotion stories above chance, which supports cross-cultural recognition of basic expressions. (2) Recognition rates varied across cultures.
- **Limitations:** basic-emotion universality is debated (social-constructivist critiques). Posed, exaggerated expressions read more reliably than subtle ones, which is useful for small mobile images.

### Faigin1990
- **Title:** The Artist's Complete Guide to Facial Expression
- **Author / year / publisher:** Gary Faigin, 1990, Watson-Guptill (New York). Later reissued by Clarkson Potter/Ten Speed.
- **ID:** ISBN 978-0-8230-0432-4. TAMU catalogue https://libcat.library.tamu.edu/Record/in00000982060/Description
- **Evidence type:** Bibliographic. Practitioner.
- **Accessed:** library records (287 pp.). Text **not read**.

### Lorenz1943
- **Title:** Die angeborenen Formen möglicher Erfahrung
- **Author / year / venue:** Konrad Lorenz, *Zeitschrift für Tierpsychologie* 5, 235–409, 1943.
- **Evidence type:** Bibliographic (classic ethology).
- **Accessed:** citation as given in Glocker et al. 2009's reference list. Original **not read**.
- **Usable findings (via Glocker 2009):** "Kindchenschema" features are a large head, high and protruding forehead, large eyes, chubby cheeks, small nose and mouth, short thick limbs and a plump body.
- **Limitations:** written in 1943 and shaped by its historical context. Use only the feature list, as summarized by later peer-reviewed work.

### Glocker2009
- **Title:** Baby Schema in Infant Faces Induces Cuteness Perception and Motivation for Caretaking in Adults
- **Authors / year / venue:** Melanie L. Glocker, Daniel D. Langleben, Kosha Ruparel, James W. Loughead, Ruben C. Gur, Norbert Sachser. *Ethology* 115(3), 257–263, 2009.
- **DOI:** 10.1111/j.1439-0310.2008.01603.x ; PDF https://www.cceb.upenn.edu/csa/assets/user-content/documents/BabySchemainInfantFacesInducesCutenessPerceptionandMotivationforCaretakinginAdults.pdf
- **Evidence type:** Empirical (experiment).
- **Accessed:** full text PDF.
- **Usable findings:** (1) Infant photographs were parametrically morphed to high baby schema (round face, high forehead, bigger eyes, smaller nose and mouth) or low. 122 undergraduates rated them. (2) High-schema faces were rated cuter (F(2,59)=137.3, p<0.001) and gave higher caretaking motivation. There was no gender effect on cuteness. (3) The authors note that the film, toy and advertising industries exploit baby schema (citing Gould 1979 on Mickey Mouse). For mascots, pushing head:body ratio, eye size and forehead height raises perceived cuteness.
- **Limitations:** real infant faces of Caucasian babies rated by US students. Generalization to drawn mascots is supported by the earlier schematic-drawing studies they cite, but was not tested here.

### Gould1979
- **Title:** Mickey Mouse Meets Konrad Lorenz (reprinted as "A Biological Homage to Mickey Mouse")
- **Author / year / venue:** Stephen Jay Gould, *Natural History* 88(5), 30–36, 1979.
- **Evidence type:** Bibliographic. Essay with informal measurements.
- **Accessed:** citation via an ECU course page and Glocker 2009. A PDF copy was listed (cognitionandculture.net) but **not read**. Ecotone 2008 reprint listing (Project MUSE).
- **Usable findings (secondary):** over about 50 years Mickey's design became progressively more juvenile (relatively larger eyes and head), a "neoteny" in design that tracked his gentler persona.
- **Limitations:** an essay, not a controlled study. The book collection it was reprinted in was not confirmed (one source says *Ever Since Darwin*; commonly cited as *The Panda's Thumb*).

### Mori2012
- **Title:** The Uncanny Valley [From the Field] (translation of Mori 1970, "Bukimi no tani", *Energy* 7(4))
- **Authors / year / venue:** Masahiro Mori; trans. Karl F. MacDorman & Norri Kageki. *IEEE Robotics & Automation Magazine* 19(2), 98–100, 2012.
- **DOI:** 10.1109/MRA.2012.2192811 ; full text https://spectrum.ieee.org/the-uncanny-valley
- **Evidence type:** Theoretical essay (not an experiment).
- **Accessed:** full translated text (IEEE Spectrum).
- **Usable findings:** (1) Affinity rises with human likeness, then drops into a "valley" for near-human entities (prosthetic hand, corpse, zombie) before the healthy-human peak. (2) Movement amplifies both peaks and valleys, and timing changes such as a slowed smile can turn a friendly expression creepy. (3) Mori's design advice is to aim for the **first peak**, a moderate human likeness with high affinity, rather than near-perfect realism. He gives stylized nonhuman designs (and eyeglasses) as examples.
- **Limitations:** a 1970 hypothesis. The empirical literature since is mixed on the curve's shape. Use it as a risk heuristic for AI-realistic faces and hands.

### ThomasJohnston1981
- **Title:** Disney Animation: The Illusion of Life
- **Authors / year / publisher:** Frank Thomas & Ollie Johnston, 1981, Abbeville Press. Later editions Hyperion/Disney Editions (1995 as *The Illusion of Life: Disney Animation*).
- **URL:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Animation:_The_Illusion_of_Life ; TAMU catalogue https://libcat.library.tamu.edu/Record/in00003583217/Description
- **Evidence type:** Bibliographic. Practitioner / documented animation history.
- **Accessed:** catalogue and dealer listings (576 pp., storyboards to full sequences) and Wikipedia. Text **not read**.
- **Usable findings:** the source of the "12 principles of animation" (squash and stretch, staging, appeal, solid drawing…). Cite it for "appeal" and staging in character work.
- **Limitations:** no confirmation from the sources accessed that it reproduces model sheets. Do not cite it for turnaround conventions.

## Thai typography rules for image work

Tags: [source key] = backed by an entry above. [craft] = Artistic practice and house judgement, not research.

1. **Never let the image model draw Thai.** Generate text-free plates and set every Thai glyph in the HTML/Chrome layer. Subword text encoders can't spell reliably, and Thai's stacked marks and rare training data make it worse. [Liu2023, inference for Thai]
2. **Budget vertical space for four above-levels and two below-levels.** A syllable can carry vowel + tone + karan above (e.g. ปั๊ม, ที่, ศักดิ์) and ุ/ู below. Line height must clear the tallest stacks of the line above and the deepest stacks of the line below. [MS-ThaiOpenType, W3C-ThaiLreq2026]
3. **Line-height defaults:** Thai body/caption copy 1.5–1.7, Thai headlines 1.25–1.4. Never below about 1.2 for Thai, even in tight display type. Latin-only headlines can go to about 1.0–1.1. Start from 1.5 (the WCAG robustness floor), then tune per font. [W3C-WCAG21-1.4.12 as floor; values are craft]
4. **Different Thai fonts have different built-in metrics** (Google targets about 130% UPM, more for tall scripts). The same `line-height` number looks different in Mitr and Noto Sans Thai, so judge by eye on the render. [GoogleFonts-Metrics]
5. **Check for clipping** at the top of the first line and the bottom of the last line: tone marks above ป ฝ ฟ and tails of ฎ ฏ. Keep `overflow` visible and add padding inside text boxes, pills and speech bubbles. [GoogleFonts-Metrics, MS-ThaiOpenType]
6. **A dotted circle ◌ in a render means invalid mark order or a wrong font.** It is a stop-ship error. [MS-ThaiOpenType]
7. **Type marks in Unicode order:** consonant → above/below vowel → tone mark. Pre-base vowels (เ แ โ ใ ไ) are typed first. Wrong order gives floating or misplaced marks that look "almost right". [Unicode18-Ch16, Ishida-ThaiOrthography]
8. **Thai has no spaces between words; spaces separate phrases.** Don't add a space between every word for "readability", because that is a different, wrong orthography. [Unicode18-Ch16, W3C-ThaiLreq2026]
9. **Control line breaks yourself in headlines.** Chrome breaks Thai with an ICU dictionary that can split names, brand words and loanwords. Set headlines line by line (`<br>` or separate spans), or insert U+200B where a break is allowed and U+2060 or `white-space: nowrap` where it isn't. [ICU-BoundaryAnalysis, Ishida-ThaiOrthography]
10. **No orphaned single syllable or lone ๆ on a line.** Keep ๆ attached to its word (it must never wrap alone). Avoid a last line with one short word: rebalance, or use `text-wrap: balance` and check the result. [Ishida-ThaiOrthography; orphan rule is craft]
11. **Keep ฯ with its word** (กรุงเทพฯ) and follow it with a space. ฯลฯ means "etc.". [Ishida-ThaiOrthography]
12. **Choose looped or loopless by context.** Looped type for longer reading, information-dense or formal and older-audience text: all age groups rated it more legible there. Loopless for short display headlines, a modern or young tone, and small UI-like labels, where younger readers did better. [MangasAfonso2025, Typotheque2025]
13. **Loopless risk: confusable glyphs** (ก/ถ/ภ, บ/ป, ข/ช, ฎ/ฏ) in very short texts, single words and prices. For one-word signs or names in a loopless font, enlarge and test, or switch to a looped face. [GoogleFonts-ThaiFamilies]
14. **Wider, open letterforms survive blur and distance better.** For Reels text read at a glance, prefer wide-proportioned faces (Mitr, Prompt, Kanit) over condensed ones. [Punsongserm2017, Punsongserm2025; family choice is craft]
15. **Project font map:** Prompt/Kanit = clean, modern promo headlines. Mitr = friendly, rounded casual posters. Noto Sans Thai = neutral UI and body text, and the safest fallback. Sriracha = informal loopless handwriting (speech bubbles). Itim = informal looped handwriting (warm notes). Mali = childlike handwriting (story carousels). All are OFL. [GoogleFonts-ThaiFamilies; mapping is craft]
16. **Minimum sizes on mobile.** On a 1080-px-wide canvas, Thai body text should be at least about 36–40 px, Thai headlines 64 px or more, and fine print never below about 28 px. Thai needs roughly 10–15% more size than Latin at the same apparent weight, because its x-height-bound letters and marks are smaller. [craft — no Thai mobile standard found]
17. **Never stretch, squash or fake-bold Thai.** Scaling distorts the thick/thin stroke logic and mark positions that the foundry tuned. Use a real weight (Prompt and Kanit ship 9 weights) and never add `-webkit-text-stroke` to fatten. [GoogleFonts-ThaiFamilies mark-position note; rule is craft]
18. **Don't letter-space Thai body text.** Tracking breaks clusters visually and pulls marks away from their bases. Small positive tracking is fine on Latin caps (e.g. the spaced TIME OPTIC wordmark). [craft; Unicode18-Ch16 notes ZWSP is ignored in letter spacing]
19. **Thai + Latin pairing:** match by the visual height of Thai body letters versus the Latin x-height, then stroke weight, then terminals (rounded with rounded, flat with flat). Prefer families designed as Thai + Latin pairs (Prompt, Kanit, Mitr, Noto Sans Thai + Noto Sans) over mixing Inter with an unrelated Thai face. If you mix, scale the Thai span (`font-size` about 1.05–1.15×) until the heights look equal. [craft; pairing intent noted in GoogleFonts-ThaiFamilies (Prompt Latin "developed to work harmoniously")]
20. **Set `lang="th"`** on Thai elements so the browser picks Thai line breaking and the right font fallback, and declare the Thai font first in the stack for Thai spans. [ICU-BoundaryAnalysis; implementation craft]
21. **Use OFL fonts for anything published.** The original SIPA licence text was not verified, so use the Google Fonts OFL revisions (Sarabun, K2D, Mali, Chakra Petch…). [GoogleFonts-ThaiFamilies, NationalFonts-SIPA]
22. **9:16 safe zone:** keep all Thai text, prices and logos out of the bottom 35% (Meta). As house margins, also keep about 14% at the top and about 6% at the sides clear (third-party figures, unconfirmed). For TikTok, overlay the official safe-zone template, which varies with caption length. [Meta-ReelsAds, TikTok-InFeedSpecs; top/side values craft]
23. **4:5 / 1:1 feed:** design at 1080×1350 (or 1440×1800 for ads) and keep text inside about 1:1 centre margins, because grid previews crop 4:5 posts. [Meta-AdsGuide-IGFeed; crop margin is craft]
24. **Contrast:** Thai strokes are thinner than Latin caps at the same size. Put Thai text on calm, low-detail areas or a scrim (use the WCAG 4.5:1 target for small text). [W3C_WCAG21; Thai stroke note craft]
25. **Proofreading checklist before export** (read the render, not the source):
    - สระ: pre-base vowels on the correct side; sara am (ำ) intact; above/below vowels not colliding.
    - วรรณยุกต์: correct tone mark (่ ้ ๊ ๋) on the right consonant, sitting above the vowel when there is one, not floating or clipped.
    - การันต์ (์): present on loanwords and names (คอนแทคเลนส์, ฟิล์ม, เลนส์), placed above the silent letter.
    - ๆ and ฯ: spacing correct, not wrapped alone.
    - No ◌ dotted circles, tofu boxes □ or Latin-fallback glyphs.
    - Line breaks fall between words; no split names or brand words; no single-syllable orphan line.
    - Numbers, prices and dates: consistent Arabic or Thai digits throughout the piece.
    - Spelling checked by a native reader for any public promo. [MS-ThaiOpenType, Ishida-ThaiOrthography; checklist is craft]

## Synthesis for an AI art director

1. **Split every deliverable into two layers.** The plate is AI-generated, text-free, with calm zones planned where type will sit. The type and brand layer is HTML. This is the only reliable way to get correct Thai and exact brand assets. [Liu2023; house rule]
2. **Write the hierarchy before the prompt.** Decide what is seen 1st, 2nd and 3rd (subject → headline → CTA/logo), then compose the plate to leave room for it. Use one dominant element; everything else should be in clear contrast or clear affinity with it. [Block2008 contrast/affinity, NNG-Pernice2019]
3. **Pick a grid before placing anything.** For 1:1/4:5, use a 4–6 column grid with outer margins of about 6–8% of the width. For 9:16, use the safe-zone rectangle as the live area. Snap headline, logo and product to the grid lines. [MullerBrockmann1981; numbers are craft]
4. **Sparse single images aren't web pages.** F and layer-cake patterns describe text-heavy pages. For posters and single images, gaze goes to the most salient element first, so build salience (contrast, faces, size) on purpose rather than relying on "reading patterns". Z-pattern layouts are craft, not eyetracking evidence. [NNG-Pernice2017, NNG-Pernice2019]
5. **For text-heavy carousels, use layer-cake design:** strong, descriptive slide headings that lead with information-carrying words, short chunks and bolded key terms. [NNG-Pernice2017, NNG-Pernice2019]
6. **Info slides: maximize signal.** Remove decorative chart ink unless it carries brand mood or memorability, and say which of the two you chose. [Tufte1983]
7. **Brand consistency is a system:** fixed palette tokens, fixed fonts, logo clear space and positions per format (partner logo top-left on clips, line art top-right on posts). Check every output against the tokens, not against memory. [Wheeler2017; project house rules]
8. **Choose the Thai face by job:** looped for reading and trust, loopless for punchy modern headlines, handwriting faces only for voice or dialogue. Then prove legibility on a phone-size preview. [MangasAfonso2025, GoogleFonts-ThaiFamilies]
9. **Every format has a hostile UI.** Keep the bottom 35% of 9:16 clear and overlay the platform template on the final frame before delivery. [Meta-ReelsAds, TikTok-InFeedSpecs]
10. **Sequence = closure.** In carousels and story clips, choose the transition type deliberately: action-to-action for plot, aspect-to-aspect for mood (slow, atmospheric), scene-to-scene for time jumps. Leave gaps the viewer can fill rather than drawing every beat. [McCloud1993]
11. **Plan shots like a cinematographer:** for each frame decide angle (objective, subjective or POV), shot size (establishing → close-up), screen direction continuity and the cut point. Write these into the prompt as camera terms. [Mascelli1965]
12. **Build intensity with contrast and calm with affinity.** Rising tension uses more tonal and colour contrast and more diagonals; resolution uses affinity (similar tones, horizontals). Map this to the emotion id of each slide or shot. [Block2008]
13. **Characters: silhouette first.** A character must read as a black shape at thumbnail size. Use shape language (round = friendly, angular = tense) as a hypothesis, not a law, and test it. [Artistic; Pixar shape-language source unverified]
14. **Cute mascots: use baby-schema levers deliberately.** Larger head-to-body ratio, larger and lower-set eyes, high forehead, small nose and mouth, rounded cheeks. Push them for warmth; pull back for credibility or adult audiences (a shop expert character). [Glocker2009, Lorenz1943, Gould1979]
15. **Expressions as muscle actions:** prompt "inner brows raised, lip corners pulled down" rather than just "sad". Exaggerate for small mobile frames, because posed basic expressions read most reliably across viewers. [EkmanFriesen1978, EkmanFriesen1971, Faigin1990]
16. **Avoid the uncanny valley.** Prefer clearly stylized characters, or fully photographic people checked for hands, eyes and teeth. Near-real AI faces with slightly wrong motion or timing are the worst case, especially in video. Mori's advice: aim for the first peak. [Mori2012]
17. **Keep characters consistent across a series** with a written character sheet: proportions, palette, costume, signature shapes and front/3-4/side references used as img2img or `-i` references every time. [Artistic; ThomasJohnston1981 for "appeal"/"solid drawing" vocabulary]
18. **Environments need landmarks and edges.** Give each recurring location one memorable landmark and a clear spatial edge, so viewers keep orientation across cuts. Add scale cues (people, doors, known objects such as glasses on a counter) to show size. [Lynch1960; scale cues are Artistic]
19. **QC every render at phone size, in this order:** text correctness (Thai checklist) → safe zones → hierarchy readable in 1 second → brand tokens → uncanny artefacts (hands, lenses, reflections). [synthesis of above]
20. **Separate evidence from taste in notes to the user:** say when a choice follows research (looped legibility, safe zones, baby schema) and when it is style. Offer alternatives (e.g. a loopless variant, a calmer plate) rather than one "correct" design. [_BRIEF honesty rules]
