**Pick (b): warm everyday moments, large type, one clear next step.**

**Why (b):**
- It shows the benefit people want (reading to a grandchild, the menu, the phone) and gives one easy action, so the audience feels able to act.
- Presbyopia is normal ageing that happens to everyone (C05), not a disease to be scared of. That makes a fear message both inaccurate and unnecessary.
- Large type and a single CTA suit older viewers (less load, readable without sound).
- A soft, inviting CTA fits Thai service politeness (เกรงใจ): "แวะมาวัดสายตาได้นะคะ" rather than "รีบมา!".

**Why not (a):** a strong threat with low efficacy leads to avoidance (EPPM, Witte 1992; average fear effects are only small to medium, Tannenbaum 2015). "ตาจะแย่ลงเรื่อยๆ" also overstates. Presbyopia does progress, but glasses correct the blur; they don't stop the ageing, so the line risks CPA ม.22 and Meta's sensational/negative self-perception rules.

**Why not (c):** a fast meme edit with slang fights older viewers' processing speed and the register they respect, and mocking age breaks Meta's rules and our comedy red lines.

**Risks of (b), and how to handle them:**
1. **It can be too bland to stop the scroll.** Open on one specific, recognisable moment (arm stretched out to read the phone) rather than a generic smile.
2. **Meta personal-attribute rule.** Never "อายุ 55 แล้วใช่ไหม / สายตาคุณ…". Talk about people in general ("หลายคนพออายุ 40 ปลายๆ…").
3. **Age stereotypes.** Not everyone 55+ is a grandparent; mix in work, hobby and travel moments. No elderspeak, no "จ้ะ".
4. **Claims.** "ตรวจวัดสายตา", not "ตรวจตา". No "รักษา". Accurate ages (NEI: usually after 45). Any blurry-vision effect gets a "ภาพจำลอง" label. Add a referral line if symptoms are mentioned.
5. **People in the images.** Real customers need written PDPA consent; AI people get the AI label and must not pose as real customers.
